# Operator guide

For control-room operators. You watch cameras, handle alerts and record what you did.

## Your screen

- **Overview**: today's events, devices and the latest alerts.
- **Alerts**: everything that needs a person. New alerts also pop up at the top of every page with a sound (the speaker button at the top turns sound on or off).
- **Playback**: pick a camera and day, click the timeline to play the recording; **Play recording** on an alert opens it 10 seconds before.
- **Live view**: pick cameras on the left; 1, 4, 9 or 16 at a time. Double-click a tile for full screen. Click a PTZ camera's tile to steer it: hold an arrow to move, + and − to zoom, or pick a preset and press Go. If someone else is steering it, wait 30 seconds.
- **Events**: everything the cameras and NVRs reported, including what didn't raise an alert.
- **Site map**: cameras, zones and guards on the site plan. Click a camera to watch it.
- **Phone app** (`/m/` on your phone, installed to the home screen): open alerts with snapshots; acknowledge, add a note, close or watch live when you're away from the desk.

## Handling an alert

1. Open it: snapshot, camera, zone and time. Critical alerts get a video clip about a minute after the event; for others, ask for one with the clip button.
2. **Acknowledge** it so others know you have it. The escalation timer stops, and any siren or light the alert switched on goes off. Switch sirens and lights by hand from the panel in Live view.
3. Look: open **Live view** or the camera on the **Site map**. Send a guard if needed.
4. Write a **note** of what you saw and did. Notes are part of the record and of any evidence.
5. **Close** it with the right reason:
   - **Real incident**: something happened. The evidence is sealed automatically.
   - **False alarm**: nothing there (an animal, shadows, rain). Places that alert again and again show in the weekly report, so the CSO can fix the rule or the camera.
   - **Test**, **Duplicate**, **Other**.

Unacknowledged critical alerts escalate to the CSO and management after the time set in the rule. Never close an alert you haven't looked at.

## Logbook and handover

- Write what happens during the shift in the **Logbook**: patrols, incidents, visitors, maintenance. You
  can correct your own entry for 15 minutes; after that, add a new entry.
- At the end of the shift, **Hand over shift**: HSS adds the summary (alerts, open alerts, faults, sirens
  still on); you add what the next shift must watch.
- The next operator sees the handover on the Overview and in the Logbook and presses **Take over**.

## Alerts you'll see

| Alert | What to do |
|---|---|
| Intrusion, line crossing | Check the camera live; send a guard; note the outcome |
| Camera offline, video loss | Check if it's back in a few minutes; report to IT if not |
| Drive fault | Report to IT at once: recordings are at risk |
| Guard SOS | Call the guard; send backup; this is always critical |
| Missed patrol | Call the guard assigned to that round |
| Zone entered (vehicle or guard) | Check who it is on the linked camera |

## Lockdown

For a serious threat (intruders, armed robbery, unrest at the gate) press the red **Lockdown** button at the top and say what is happening. Every alert rule is armed, every alert becomes critical, the lockdown lights and sirens come on, and the CSO, management and IT are messaged. Then follow the emergency plan and call the police. Only the CSO or an administrator can end it.

## Good practice

- What you watch, live or recorded, is recorded in the viewing log with your name. Watch cameras for security work only.

- Sign out when you leave the desk. Never share your account.
- Don't film the screen with a phone or send pictures outside the system. Evidence goes through the vault.
- Face and plate matches (if switched on) are leads, not facts. Compare the images, then call the CSO. Never stop anyone on a match alone.
